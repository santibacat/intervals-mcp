# Planning guardrails

Apply these as conservative review rules, not as medical or universal coaching laws. Calibrate decisions against the athlete's own recent history and goal.

## Required context

- Recent 6-12 week volume and frequency per sport.
- Current zones or thresholds and their measurement dates.
- Event type, date, priority, terrain, elevation, and expected duration.
- Weekly availability and preferred long-session days.
- Recent injury, illness, pain, fatigue, sleep disruption, and life stress.
- Equipment and environment constraints.

If key context is absent, reduce specificity and surface the uncertainty.

## Weekly review

- Separate load and progression by running, cycling, swimming, and strength.
- Avoid abrupt running-volume increases; use recent tolerated volume rather than a universal percentage.
- Avoid consecutive key sessions unless deliberately justified and historically tolerated.
- Protect recovery after long runs, long rides, demanding bricks, races, and heavy lower-body strength.
- Include at least one low-load/rest opportunity in the week.
- Keep easy sessions genuinely easy.
- Check that the long run is proportionate to recent weekly running volume.
- Schedule recovery weeks and event-specific taper rather than increasing load indefinitely.
- Treat HRV, resting HR, sleep, readiness, soreness, and subjective fatigue as contextual trends, not single-signal commands.

## Stop conditions

Do not produce a normal progression when the athlete reports acute pain, suspected injury, significant illness, chest symptoms, fainting, or other red flags. Recommend pausing the plan and seeking an appropriate qualified professional. Do not diagnose or prescribe treatment.

## Final plan report

Report totals per sport, number of demanding sessions, longest session, planned recovery, progression versus recent history, and every unresolved warning. The athlete must understand why each key session exists before applying the plan.
